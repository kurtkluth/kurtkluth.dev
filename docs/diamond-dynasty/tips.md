Twelve habits that turn close losses into wins. None of them require luck,
just doing them on purpose.

## Wait for the ball, do not lunge at it

The single biggest jump in hitting is patience with your swing. Let the
ball travel and swing as the gold ring lands on it, not when you first
see it leave the hand. Early swings foul off or roll over; a swing timed
to the arrival is what drives the ball.

## Learn the difference between late and unreachable

After every swing the game tells you what went wrong. **EARLY** or **LATE**
with a number is a timing problem, and the fix is the clock. **OUT OF
REACH** means your timing was fine and the bat was simply never going to
get there, and the fix is to stop swinging at that pitch. They are
different mistakes with different cures, so read the message.

## Lay off the chase

A pitch outside the white zone is a trap. Contact is decided by where your
barrel actually is, so a ball off the plate is not a weak hit waiting to
happen, it is a swing and a miss. Take the free ones, work the count, and
make the pitcher come to you. Four balls is a base without a single swing.

## Steal with your burners, not with everybody

The send button appears whenever a runner has somewhere to go, but it is
not always the right call. Speed against a weak armed catcher is close to
free; your slowest bat with two out is how you end an inning you were
about to win. Check who is actually on base.

## Steal the first pitch when you are pitching

Hitters take a lot of first pitches. An Attack call on 0-0 is close to a
free strike, and pitching from ahead is worth more than anything clever you
could do later. Save the corners for when you have earned them.

## Never nibble on 3-0

At 3-0 the hitter is not swinging at anything. A corner or a waste pitch is
just a walk with extra steps. Throw the most hittable pitch you own and
take the strike.

## Waste pitches only work with two strikes

That is the one count where a protecting hitter will chase. On any other
count a waste pitch is a ball you handed over for nothing.

## Do not repeat yourself

Throw the same pitch three times in a row and the hitter starts sitting on
it, and the game warns you with a ring around the button. Change speeds.

## Respect the TIRED tag, on both mounds

A tired pitcher is not slightly worse, they are a batting practice machine:
worse command, and slower pitches that are far easier to time. Plan your
one bullpen call before the damage, not after. And when the opposing
starter is deep in the TIRED zone and the AI has not pulled them yet, that
is the moment to attack everything hittable. The window closes the instant
their bullpen door opens.

## Take the fielding chance every time

When a hit drops in, you get a shot at turning it into an out. Missing it
costs nothing at all, because the play just stands as it was called. There
is never a reason not to try.

## Buy facilities before your fifth pack

A pack is 200 coins of maybe. The Fan Center pays a percentage of every
match you will ever play again, and the Analytics Department tells you what
is coming before it is thrown. Once your lineup is respectable, permanent
upgrades and targeted training on high potential starters both beat another
spin of the odds.

## Train potential, not nostalgia

Training coins grow a player toward their potential, so a high potential
young card out-earns a maxed veteran over time. Check potential before
you spend; the best investment is rarely the current best player.

## Auto-set, then second-guess it

The lineup auto set places your best nine instantly and is right most of
the time. Glance over it anyway after every pack; a new arrival can slot
in the moment the flip animation ends.
